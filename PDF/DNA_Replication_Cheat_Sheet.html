Replication Sequence — Prokaryotic (E. coli)



1. DnaA-ATP binds oriC → AT-rich region melts
  • DnaC loads DnaB helicase (2 hexamers)

  • DnaG primase synthesizes RNA primers

  • Clamp loader loads β sliding clamp

  • Pol III synthesizes DNA:

    •    • Leading strand: continuous, one primer
       • Lagging strand: Okazaki fragments (1-2 kb), multiple primers
  • Proofreading: ε subunit (3'→5' exonuclease)

  • Pol I: removes RNA primers, fills gaps

  • DNA ligase: seals nicks

  • Gyrase: relieves supercoiling ahead of fork

  • Tus/Ter: termination at Ter region

  • Topo IV: decatenates daughter chromosomes



    • Replication Sequence — Eukaryotic (Human)



    G1 PHASE (LICENSING):
  • ORC binds origins

  • Cdc6 + Cdt1 recruited

  • MCM2-7 double hexamers loaded → Origin licensed


    • S PHASE (ACTIVATION):
  • CDK + DDK phosphorylate MCM

  • Cdc45 + GINS loaded → CMG helicase active

  • CMG unwinds DNA

  • RPA binds ssDNA

  • Pol α/primase makes RNA-DNA primer

  • RFC loads PCNA

  • Polymerase switching: Pol α → Pol ε (leading) or Pol δ (lagging)

  • Leading: Pol ε continuous; Lagging: Pol δ, Okazaki fragments (100-200 nt)


    • PROCESSING:
  • RNase H2 removes RNA primers

  • FEN1 removes flaps

  • Pol δ fills gaps

  • DNA Ligase I seals nicks

  • CAF-1 reassembles nucleosomes


    • TERMINATION:
  • Forks converge → CMG disassembled (p97/Cdc48)

  • Gaps filled → ligation


    • TELOMERES:
  • Telomerase extends 3' overhang (TERT + TERC)

  • Complementary strand filled by Pol α/Pol δ

    Telomere Quick Facts



  • Sequence: TTAGGG (human), repeated 2,000-3,000 times

  • Length: 5-15 kb at birth; shortens ~50-200 bp/division

  • Enzyme: Telomerase = TERT (protein) + TERC (RNA template)

  • Template in TERC: 3'-CAAUCCCAAUC-5'

  • Shelterin: TRF1, TRF2, POT1, TIN2, TPP1, RAP1

  • Cancer: 85-90% reactivate telomerase; 10-15% use ALT

  • Aging: Short telomeres → senescence (Hayflick limit ~50-60 divisions)

    Important Exam Facts



  • DNA is always synthesized 5'→3' (NO exceptions)

  • Both strands are synthesized 5'→3' (lagging strand = Okazaki fragments)

  • DNA polymerase cannot start synthesis without a primer

  • E. coli ligase uses NAD⁺; eukaryotic ligase uses ATP

  • The sliding clamp increases processivity from ~20 to >50,000 nt

  • E. coli has one origin (oriC); humans have ~30,000-50,000

  • Human Okazaki fragments: 100-200 nt; E. coli: 1-2 kb

  • Telomerase is a reverse transcriptase (uses RNA template)

  • Fluoroquinolones target DNA gyrase (bacteria-specific)

  • Lynch syndrome = defective mismatch repair → MSI

  • Proofreading improves fidelity 100-fold (10⁻⁵ → 10⁻⁷)

  • MMR improves fidelity another 100-1000-fold (10⁻⁷ → 10⁻⁹–10⁻¹⁰)

  • Licensing (G1) and firing (S) are mutually exclusive (CDK)

  • PCNA is a trimer; β clamp is a dimer

  • Fork speed: E. coli ~1,000 nt/s; Human ~50 nt/s




    • Common Mistakes to Avoid



    MistakeCorrection
    --------------------
    "Lagging strand is synthesized 3'→5'"Both strands are 5'→3'
    "DNA polymerase starts from scratch"Needs a primer (3'-OH)
    "Okazaki fragments are the same size in all organisms"E. coli: 1-2 kb; Human: 100-200 bp
    "Proofreading is the main fidelity mechanism"All three layers contribute equally important
    "Telomerase is active in all cells"Inactive in most somatic cells
    "Both strands need the same number of primers"Leading: 1 primer; Lagging: many
    "Replication and transcription use the same polymerase"Completely different polymerases
    "PCR is the same as cellular replication"PCR uses heat denaturation, not helicase
