DNA Replication — Long Answer / Essay Questions

15 Questions (8-12 marks each)

Question 1 (10 marks)

Describe the complete mechanism of DNA replication initiation in E. coli, from DnaA binding to the formation of two functional replication forks. Include the roles of all major proteins involved.

Expected content: DnaA-ATP, DnaA boxes, AT-rich melting, DnaC/DnaB loading, bidirectional fork formation, helicase activation

Question 2 (10 marks)

Compare and contrast the replication mechanisms of E. coli and human cells. Include at least eight specific comparisons covering initiation, elongation, termination, and regulation.

Expected content: Origins, helicases, polymerases, sliding clamps, Okazaki fragment size, telomeres, licensing, chromatin, speed

Question 3 (12 marks)

Explain the concept of replication licensing in eukaryotic cells. Describe the molecular events that occur during G1 licensing and S-phase activation. Why is this system essential for maintaining genome stability? What happens when it fails?

Expected content: Pre-RC assembly, ORC/Cdc6/Cdt1/MCM, CDK regulation, geminin, once-per-cycle firing, re-replication consequences

Question 4 (10 marks)

Describe the three layers of replication fidelity in E. coli. Explain the molecular mechanism at each level and calculate the overall error rate. What are the clinical consequences of defects in each layer?

Expected content: Base selection, proofreading, MMR, error rates, Lynch syndrome, cancer predisposition

Question 5 (10 marks)

Explain the end-replication problem in detail. How do telomeres and telomerase address this problem? Describe the structure and mechanism of telomerase and discuss its role in aging and cancer.

Expected content: Linear chromosome problem, primer removal, progressive shortening, telomerase structure (TERT/TERC), extension mechanism, aging, cancer, ALT

Question 6 (10 marks)

Describe the structure and function of the eukaryotic replisome during S phase. Include the roles of CMG helicase, RPA, Pol α/primase, Pol ε, Pol δ, PCNA, and RFC. How are these components coordinated?

Expected content: CMG activation, polymerase switching, leading/lagging strand synthesis, Okazaki fragment processing

Question 7 (12 marks)

Explain the ATR-CHK1 checkpoint pathway in response to replication stress. What types of stress activate this pathway? What are the downstream effects? How does this pathway relate to cancer development and therapy?

Expected content: ssDNA/RPA, ATRIP recruitment, CHK1 activation, fork stabilization, origin suppression, cancer relevance, therapeutic targeting

Question 8 (10 marks)

Discuss the role of mismatch repair in maintaining genome stability. Compare bacterial (MutHLS) and eukaryotic (MutSα/MutLα) mismatch repair systems. Explain the molecular basis of Lynch syndrome and describe how microsatellite instability is diagnosed.

Expected content: MMR mechanism, strand discrimination (methylation vs nicks), Lynch syndrome, MSI testing, cancer predisposition

Question 9 (10 marks)

Explain how chromatin is disassembled and reassembled during eukaryotic DNA replication. What are the roles of FACT, CAF-1, and ASF1? How are parental histone marks inherited by daughter chromosomes?

Expected content: Nucleosome disassembly, histone chaperones, recycling, reassembly, epigenetic inheritance, replication timing

Question 10 (12 marks)

Describe the molecular mechanisms by which replication stress contributes to genome instability and cancer. Include discussions of fork stalling, fork reversal, replication-transcription conflicts, and common fragile sites.

Expected content: Causes of stress, fork dynamics, R-loops, fragile sites, oncogene-induced stress, genome instability cascade

Question 11 (10 marks)

Explain the mechanism of translesion synthesis and template switching. How does the cell decide between these two pathways? What are the consequences of each for genome stability? Give specific examples of TLS polymerases and their functions.

Expected content: PCNA ubiquitylation, Pol η/ι/κ, error-prone vs error-free, lesion bypass, mutagenesis

Question 12 (10 marks)

Compare and contrast the Tus/Ter termination system in E. coli with the fork convergence termination mechanism in eukaryotes. What happens to the replisome after termination? How are daughter chromosomes separated?

Expected content: Tus/Ter polar trap, CMG disassembly (p97), decatenation (Topo II), chromosome segregation

Question 13 (8 marks)

Explain how different antibiotics and anticancer drugs target DNA replication. Include at least four specific drug targets and describe the mechanism of action for each.

Expected content: Fluoroquinolones (gyrase), PARP inhibitors (synthetic lethality), nucleoside analogs (chain termination), ATR/CHK1 inhibitors

Question 14 (10 marks)

Discuss the evolution of DNA replication machinery from prokaryotes to eukaryotes. What features are conserved? What features are unique to each system? How does the archaeal replication system relate to both?

Expected content: Conservation of sliding clamp, clamp loader, helicase family, polymerases; differences in origin structure, licensing, chromatin

Question 15 (12 marks)

A researcher discovers a new genetic disease characterized by premature aging, bone marrow failure, and increased cancer risk. Patient cells show extremely short telomeres and mutations in a protein that interacts with telomerase. Design a comprehensive experimental plan to characterize this disease, including molecular, cellular, and clinical approaches.

Expected content: Telomere length measurement, telomerase activity assays, gene identification, functional studies, mouse models, potential therapies
