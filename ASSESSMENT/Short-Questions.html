DNA Replication — Short Answer Questions

25 Questions (2-5 marks each)

Question 1 (3 marks)

Explain why DNA replication is described as "semiconservative." What would you expect to see after two rounds of replication in the Meselson-Stahl experiment if replication were conservative instead?

Question 2 (3 marks)

List the three layers of replication fidelity in E. coli and explain how each contributes to overall accuracy.

Question 3 (3 marks)

Why does DNA polymerase require a primer? Explain the chemical basis for this requirement.

Question 4 (3 marks)

Compare the structure and function of the β sliding clamp (E. coli) and PCNA (eukaryotes).

Question 5 (4 marks)

Describe the role of DnaA protein in E. coli replication initiation. What happens if DnaA cannot bind ATP?

Question 6 (3 marks)

Explain why the lagging strand is synthesized discontinuously. What would happen if both strands could be synthesized continuously?

Question 7 (4 marks)

Describe the function of DNA polymerase I in E. coli. Why is it unique among E. coli polymerases?

Question 8 (3 marks)

What is the role of DNA gyrase in replication? How do fluoroquinolone antibiotics exploit this?

Question 9 (4 marks)

Explain the concept of replication licensing. Why is it important for genome stability?

Question 10 (3 marks)

Describe the differences between Pol ε and Pol δ in terms of their roles during eukaryotic replication.

Question 11 (4 marks)

Explain the end-replication problem. Why does it affect linear chromosomes but not circular chromosomes?

Question 12 (3 marks)

What is the role of telomerase in maintaining telomere length? Name its two essential components.

Question 13 (4 marks)

Explain how the ATR-CHK1 checkpoint pathway responds to replication stress.

Question 14 (3 marks)

Compare the mismatch repair systems in E. coli and human cells. What is the key difference in strand discrimination?

Question 15 (4 marks)

Describe how Okazaki fragments are processed in eukaryotes. Name the enzymes involved at each step.

Question 16 (3 marks)

What is chromatin remodeling during replication? Why is it necessary in eukaryotes but not prokaryotes?

Question 17 (3 marks)

Explain the relationship between CDK activity and replication licensing. Why are these mutually exclusive?

Question 18 (4 marks)

A researcher discovers a new protein that, when mutated, causes cells to accumulate reversed replication forks. What protein might this be, and what is its normal function?

Question 19 (3 marks)

Describe the mechanism by which translesion synthesis bypasses DNA lesions.

Question 20 (4 marks)

Explain how oncogene activation leads to replication stress. Give two specific examples of oncogenes and their mechanisms.

Question 21 (3 marks)

What is the role of the Shelterin complex at telomeres? Name three of its components.

Question 22 (3 marks)

Compare the replication speeds of E. coli and human cells. Explain why they differ.

Question 23 (4 marks)

Describe the complete pathway from origin recognition to fork activation in eukaryotic cells.

Question 24 (3 marks)

How does the Fanconi anemia pathway relate to DNA replication? What happens when it is defective?

Question 25 (3 marks)

Explain the concept of synthetic lethality and how it is applied in cancer therapy using PARP inhibitors.

Suggested Marking Scheme

3-mark questions: 1 mark for each key point 4-mark questions: 1 mark for each key point + clarity marks

Key Points for Sample Answers

Q1 (Semiconservative):

  • Each daughter = 1 old + 1 new strand (1)
  • Conservative: would show heavy + light bands (not hybrid) (1)
  • Dispersive: would show intermediate density bands (1)

Q9 (Licensing):

  • ORC/Cdc6/Cdt1/MCM assembly (1)
  • G1 phase (low CDK) (1)
  • Once per cycle (1)
  • Prevents re-replication (1)

Q11 (End-replication):

  • RNA primer removal at 5' end (1)
  • No 3'-OH available to fill gap (1)
  • Progressive shortening (1)
  • Circular = no ends (1)
