# DNA Replication — Viva Voce Questions

**30 Questions for Oral Examination**

---

## Basic Concepts (1-8)

**1. What is DNA replication and why is it essential for life?**
- *Expected answer*: Semiconservative copying of genome before cell division; ensures each daughter cell gets complete genetic information

**2. What are the three models of DNA replication? Which was proven correct?**
- *Expected answer*: Conservative, semiconservative, dispersive. Semiconservative proven by Meselson-Stahl (1958)

**3. Can DNA polymerase synthesize DNA in the 3'→5' direction? Why or why not?**
- *Expected answer*: No. Chemical mechanism requires 3'-OH for nucleophilic attack on incoming dNTP. Universal rule in all organisms.

**4. What is the difference between a leading strand and a lagging strand?**
- *Expected answer*: Both synthesized 5'→3'. Leading: continuous toward fork. Lagging: discontinuous (Okazaki fragments) away from fork.

**5. Why do we need RNA primers for DNA replication?**
- *Expected answer*: DNA polymerase cannot initiate synthesis de novo; needs free 3'-OH. Primase provides this with RNA primer.

**6. What is the sliding clamp and why is it important?**
- *Expected answer*: Ring-shaped protein (β₂ in bacteria, PCNA in eukaryotes) that tethers polymerase to template. Increases processivity from ~20 to >50,000 nt.

**7. What is proofreading and how does it improve replication fidelity?**
- *Expected answer*: 3'→5' exonuclease activity of polymerase removes misincorporated nucleotides. Improves fidelity 100-fold (10⁻⁵ to 10⁻⁷).

**8. What is the overall error rate of DNA replication after all correction mechanisms?**
- *Expected answer*: ~10⁻⁹ to 10⁻¹⁰ per nucleotide per cell division. Three layers: selection (10⁻⁵), proofreading (10⁻⁷), MMR (10⁻⁹–10⁻¹⁰)

## Prokaryotic Replication (9-15)

**9. Describe the structure and function of oriC.**
- *Expected answer*: 245 bp origin; DnaA boxes (9-mers) for DnaA binding; AT-rich 13-mers for melting. Single origin in E. coli.

**10. What is the role of DnaA in replication initiation?**
- *Expected answer*: Initiator protein. DnaA-ATP binds DnaA boxes cooperatively, induces positive supercoiling, melts AT-rich region.

**11. Explain how DnaB helicase is loaded onto DNA.**
- *Expected answer*: DnaC holds DnaB inactive → delivers to melted origin → DnaB encircles ssDNA → DnaC dissociates (ATP-dependent)

**12. What is unique about DNA Pol I compared to other E. coli polymerases?**
- *Expected answer*: Has 5'→3' exonuclease activity for RNA primer removal. Also has polymerase and 3'→5' exonuclease (proofreading).

**13. Why does DNA ligase in E. coli use NAD⁺ instead of ATP?**
- *Expected answer*: Biochemical difference from eukaryotes. Same mechanism (adenylation) but different cofactor. Eukaryotic/viral ligases use ATP.

**14. How does the Tus/Ter system ensure termination occurs in the correct region?**
- *Expected answer*: Tus binds Ter sequences → polar fork barrier → blocks fork in one direction but not other → traps both forks in termination region.

**15. What would happen if DNA gyrase were inactivated in E. coli?**
- *Expected answer*: Positive supercoils accumulate ahead of fork → fork stalling → DNA breakage → cell death. Gyrase is essential.

## Eukaryotic Replication (16-22)

**16. What is replication licensing and when does it occur?**
- *Expected answer*: Assembly of pre-RC (ORC→Cdc6→Cdt1→MCM) in G1 when CDK low. Ensures each origin fires once per cell cycle.

**17. Why do eukaryotes need multiple origins of replication?**
- *Expected answer*: Large genome (3.2 × 10⁹ bp); single origin would take weeks. Multiple origins (~30,000-50,000) allow replication in 6-8 hours.

**18. What is the CMG helicase and how is it activated?**
- *Expected answer*: Cdc45-MCM2-7-GINS. Inactive MCM loaded at origins → DDK + CDK phosphorylate → Cdc45 and GINS bind → active helicase.

**19. Name the three major eukaryotic replicative polymerases and their functions.**
- *Expected answer*: Pol α/primase (initiates), Pol ε (leading strand), Pol δ (lagging strand + repair)

**20. What is the role of PCNA in mismatch repair?**
- *Expected answer*: RFC loads PCNA in oriented manner; MutLα recognizes PCNA orientation → nicks new strand → directs repair to correct strand.

**21. How are Okazaki fragments processed in eukaryotes?**
- *Expected answer*: RNase H2 removes most RNA → FEN1 removes remaining RNA + flaps → Pol δ fills gaps → DNA Ligase I seals nicks

**22. What is the difference between early and late replication in S phase?**
- *Expected answer*: Euchromatin (active genes) replicates early; heterochromatin replicates late. Correlates with gene expression and epigenetic state.

## Telomeres and Checkpoints (23-27)

**23. Explain the end-replication problem in one sentence.**
- *Expected answer*: Linear chromosomes cannot fully replicate at 5' ends because RNA primers at the terminus are removed but cannot be replaced.

**24. What are the two essential components of telomerase?**
- *Expected answer*: TERT (telomerase reverse transcriptase, protein) and TERC (telomerase RNA component, contains template)

**25. Why do most somatic cells not express telomerase?**
- *Expected answer*: Telomere shortening acts as a biological clock limiting cell divisions (tumor suppression). Stem cells and germ cells maintain telomerase.

**26. How is the ATR-CHK1 checkpoint activated?**
- *Expected answer*: Stalled fork → ssDNA accumulates → RPA binds → ATRIP recruits ATR → ATR phosphorylates CHK1 → fork stabilization, origin suppression

**27. What is the clinical significance of the TERT C228T promoter mutation?**
- *Expected answer*: Creates ETS binding site → increased TERT transcription → telomerase reactivation → immortalization → found in many cancers

## Applications and Disease (28-30)

**28. How do fluoroquinolone antibiotics target bacterial replication?**
- *Expected answer*: Trap gyrase-DNA complex → stabilize DSBs → fork collapse → cell death. Selective for bacterial gyrase over human topoisomerases.

**29. Explain how PARP inhibitors work in BRCA-mutant cancers.**
- *Expected answer*: Synthetic lethality. BRCA-mutant cells lack HR; PARP inhibition prevents SSB repair → SSBs become DSBs → cannot repair → cell death.

**30. A patient has microsatellite instability in their colorectal cancer. What gene mutations would you suspect?**
- *Expected answer*: Mismatch repair genes (MLH1, MSH2, MSH6, PMS2). Likely Lynch syndrome (HNPCC). Recommend genetic testing and family screening.

## Bonus Questions (31-33)

**31. What would happen if all DnaA in E. coli were replaced with a mutant that cannot hydrolyze ATP?**
- *Expected answer*: DnaA-ATP would accumulate → continuous origin firing → re-replication → gene amplification → genome instability → likely lethal

**32. How does the cell prevent the lagging strand Pol III from continuously falling off the template?**
- *Expected answer*: β sliding clamp increases processivity; trombone model coordinates with leading strand; τ subunit links both polymerases

**33. A research lab discovers a protein that binds specifically to replication forks and promotes fork reversal. What would be the phenotype if this protein were knocked out?**
- *Expected answer*: Reduced fork reversal → increased fork collapse → more DSBs → genome instability → sensitivity to replication stress
