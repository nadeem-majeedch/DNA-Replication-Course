DNA Replication — Multiple Choice Questions

40 Questions with Answers and Explanations

Questions 1-10: Fundamentals

1. The Meselson-Stahl experiment demonstrated that DNA replication is:

  • A) Conservative
  • B) Semiconservative
  • C) Dispersive
  • D) Discontinuous

✅ Answer: B — After one generation in ¹⁴N, all DNA was hybrid density (one ¹⁵N strand, one ¹⁴N strand), confirming semiconservative replication.

2. DNA polymerase can synthesize DNA in which direction?

  • A) 3'→5' only
  • B) 5'→3' only
  • C) Both 3'→5' and 5'→3'
  • D) Direction depends on the template

✅ Answer: B — DNA polymerase catalyzes nucleophilic attack by 3'-OH on the α-phosphate of incoming dNTP. This reaction only works in the 5'→3' direction. No known exception exists.

3. Why does DNA replication require a primer?

  • A) To provide energy for the reaction
  • B) To stabilize the DNA helix
  • C) To provide a free 3'-OH group for DNA polymerase
  • D) To recruit helicase to the origin

✅ Answer: C — DNA polymerase cannot initiate synthesis de novo. It requires a pre-existing 3'-OH group to extend from. Primase synthesizes RNA primers to provide this.

4. In the lagging strand, Okazaki fragments are oriented:

  • A) 3'→5' relative to fork movement
  • B) 5'→3' away from the fork
  • C) 5'→3' toward the fork
  • D) Randomly

✅ Answer: B — Each Okazaki fragment is synthesized 5'→3' (always), but the overall direction of lagging strand synthesis is away from the replication fork.

5. The sliding clamp in E. coli is:

  • A) A monomer that binds DNA
  • B) A homodimer forming a ring
  • C) A homotrimer forming a ring
  • D) A hexamer that unwinds DNA

✅ Answer: B — The β sliding clamp is a homodimer (β₂) forming a toroidal ring. The eukaryotic equivalent (PCNA) is a homotrimer.

6. Which E. coli polymerase has 5'→3' exonuclease activity?

  • A) Pol I
  • B) Pol II
  • C) Pol III
  • D) All of them

✅ Answer: A — Pol I is unique among E. coli polymerases in having 5'→3' exonuclease activity, which is essential for removing RNA primers.

7. DNA gyrase is unique because it:

  • A) Can only relax positive supercoils
  • B) Can introduce negative supercoils using ATP
  • C) Is a type I topoisomerase
  • D) Is found in both bacteria and humans

✅ Answer: B — DNA gyrase is the only topoisomerase that can introduce negative supercoils. It uses ATP and creates a double-strand break to pass another segment through.

8. The end-replication problem affects:

  • A) Both linear and circular chromosomes equally
  • B) Only circular chromosomes
  • C) Only linear chromosomes
  • D) Prokaryotic chromosomes only

✅ Answer: C — Linear chromosomes cannot fully replicate at their 5' ends because RNA primers at the lagging strand terminus are removed but cannot be replaced. Circular chromosomes don't have ends.

9. In E. coli, the origin of replication is called:

  • A) ARS
  • B) oriC
  • C) ORC
  • D) TER

✅ Answer: B — oriC is the single origin in E. coli. ARS (Autonomously Replicating Sequence) is used in yeast; ORC is the origin recognition complex in eukaryotes.

10. Semiconservative replication was proven by:

  • A) Watson and Crick
  • B) Kornberg
  • C) Meselson and Stahl
  • D) Okazaki

✅ Answer: C — Meselson and Stahl (1958) used ¹⁵N/¹⁴N density gradient centrifugation to demonstrate semiconservative replication.

Questions 11-20: Prokaryotic Replication

11. DnaA protein functions in:

  • A) Unwinding DNA at the replication fork
  • B) Synthesizing RNA primers
  • C) Initiating replication at oriC
  • D) Joining Okazaki fragments

✅ Answer: C — DnaA-ATP binds DnaA boxes at oriC and induces melting of the AT-rich region, initiating replication.

12. DnaB helicase moves in which direction on DNA?

  • A) 3'→5'
  • B) 5'→3'
  • C) Bidirectionally
  • D) Direction depends on strand

✅ Answer: B — DnaB is a 5'→3' helicase. It encircles the lagging strand template and translocates 5'→3' to unwind DNA.

13. The function of DnaC is to:

  • A) Synthesize RNA primers
  • B) Load DnaB helicase onto DNA
  • C) Remove RNA primers
  • D) Seal nicks between Okazaki fragments

✅ Answer: B — DnaC is a helicase loader that delivers DnaB to single-stranded DNA at the origin. It holds DnaB inactive during loading, then dissociates.

14. The τ (tau) subunit of Pol III is important for:

  • A) Proofreading
  • B) Dimerizing two Pol III cores (one per strand)
  • C) Loading the β clamp
  • D) Removing RNA primers

✅ Answer: B — τ subunit dimerizes two Pol III core complexes and connects them to DnaB helicase, coordinating leading and lagging strand synthesis.

15. What is the size of Okazaki fragments in E. coli?

  • A) 100-200 nt
  • B) 500-700 nt
  • C) 1,000-2,000 nt
  • D) 5,000-10,000 nt

✅ Answer: C — E. coli Okazaki fragments are 1,000-2,000 nt. In eukaryotes, they are 100-200 nt.

16. In E. coli mismatch repair, strand discrimination is based on:

  • A) Strand breaks at Okazaki fragment junctions
  • B) Dam methylation of GATC sequences
  • C) PCNA orientation
  • D) Histone modification

✅ Answer: B — E. coli uses Dam methylation. The parental strand is methylated; the new strand is temporarily unmethylated (hemimethylated). MutH nicks the unmethylated strand.

17. The Tus/Ter system in E. coli functions to:

  • A) Initiate replication
  • B) Increase fork speed
  • C) Trap replication forks at termination
  • D) Remove RNA primers

✅ Answer: C — Tus binds Ter sequences and creates a polar fork barrier, trapping converging forks in the termination region.

18. DNA ligase in E. coli uses which cofactor?

  • A) ATP
  • B) GTP
  • C) NAD⁺
  • D) FAD

✅ Answer: C — E. coli DNA ligase uses NAD⁺. Eukaryotic DNA ligases use ATP. This is a key biochemical difference.

19. The "trombone model" describes:

  • A) How helicase moves along DNA
  • B) How the lagging strand template loops to coordinate synthesis with the leading strand
  • C) How primase interacts with DnaB
  • D) How topoisomerase relieves supercoiling

✅ Answer: B — The trombone model describes how the lagging strand template loops back so that both Pol III cores at the fork can physically move in the same direction.

20. DNA gyrase is inhibited by:

  • A) Rifampicin
  • B) Fluoroquinolones (ciprofloxacin)
  • C) Ampicillin
  • D) Tetracycline

✅ Answer: B — Fluoroquinolones trap the gyrase-DNA covalent complex, causing lethal double-strand breaks. Rifampicin targets RNA polymerase.

Questions 21-30: Eukaryotic Replication

21. The eukaryotic sliding clamp is:

  • A) β₂ homodimer
  • B) PCNA homotrimer
  • C) RFC pentamer
  • D) MCM2-7 hexamer

✅ Answer: B — PCNA (Proliferating Cell Nuclear Antigen) is a homotrimer forming a ring, similar in function to the bacterial β₂ dimer.

22. Which eukaryotic polymerase synthesizes the leading strand?

  • A) Pol α
  • B) Pol δ
  • C) Pol ε
  • D) Pol γ

✅ Answer: C — Pol ε is the primary leading strand polymerase. Pol δ synthesizes the lagging strand. Pol α/primase initiates synthesis.

23. What is the function of geminin?

  • A) Activates origins in S phase
  • B) Inhibits Cdt1 to prevent re-replication
  • C) Loads MCM helicase
  • D) Phosphorylates CDK substrates

✅ Answer: B — Geminin binds and inhibits Cdt1 in S/G2 phase, preventing new MCM loading and thus preventing re-replication.

24. The CMG helicase consists of:

  • A) DnaB + DnaC + SSB
  • B) Cdc45 + MCM2-7 + GINS
  • C) ORC + Cdc6 + Cdt1
  • D) Pol ε + PCNA + RFC

✅ Answer: B — CMG = Cdc45-MCM-GINS. It is the active replicative helicase in eukaryotes, formed when Cdc45 and GINS bind phosphorylated MCM2-7.

25. PCNA is loaded onto DNA by:

  • A) Pol α/primase
  • B) RPA
  • C) RFC (Replication Factor C)
  • D) MCM2-7

✅ Answer: C — RFC (clamp loader) uses ATP hydrolysis to open the PCNA ring and load it onto primer-template junctions.

26. RNase H2 in eukaryotes functions to:

  • A) Remove DNA mismatches
  • B) Remove RNA primers during Okazaki fragment processing
  • C) Unwind DNA at the replication fork
  • D) Synthesize RNA primers

✅ Answer: B — RNase H2 degrades most of the RNA primer, leaving 1-2 ribonucleotides that are then removed by FEN1.

27. Replication licensing occurs in which cell cycle phase?

  • A) S phase
  • B) G2 phase
  • C) M phase
  • D) G1 phase

✅ Answer: D — Licensing occurs in G1 when CDK activity is low. Pre-RC assembly (ORC → Cdc6 → Cdt1 → MCM) can only occur when CDK is inactive.

28. How many origins of replication does a human cell have approximately?

  • A) 1
  • B) 100
  • C) 30,000-50,000
  • D) 1,000,000

✅ Answer: C — Human cells have approximately 30,000-50,000 origins. This allows the large genome to be replicated in ~6-8 hours.

29. The size of Okazaki fragments in human cells is approximately:

  • A) 1,000-2,000 nt
  • B) 500-1,000 nt
  • C) 100-200 nt
  • D) 50-100 nt

✅ Answer: C — Human Okazaki fragments are 100-200 nt, much shorter than E. coli's 1-2 kb fragments (due to nucleosome obstacles).

30. FEN1 functions in:

  • A) Unwinding DNA
  • B) Removing RNA primers and DNA flaps
  • C) Loading PCNA
  • D) Synthesizing RNA primers

✅ Answer: B — FEN1 (Flap Endonuclease 1) removes 5' flap structures created during Okazaki fragment processing after Pol δ displacement synthesis.

Questions 31-40: Advanced Topics

31. Lynch syndrome is caused by mutations in:

  • A) BRCA1/BRCA2
  • B) MLH1/MSH2/MSH6/PMS2
  • C) TP53
  • D) APC

✅ Answer: B — Lynch syndrome (HNPCC) is caused by germline mutations in mismatch repair genes (MLH1, MSH2, MSH6, PMS2), leading to microsatellite instability.

32. Telomerase is:

  • A) A DNA-dependent DNA polymerase
  • B) An RNA-dependent DNA polymerase (reverse transcriptase)
  • C) An RNA-dependent RNA polymerase
  • D) A DNA-dependent RNA polymerase

✅ Answer: B — Telomerase (TERT) is a reverse transcriptase that uses its RNA component (TERC) as a template to synthesize telomeric DNA.

33. The TERT promoter mutations found in cancer:

  • A) Inactivate telomerase
  • B) Create new transcription factor binding sites → increase TERT expression
  • C) Change the telomere repeat sequence
  • D) Delete the entire TERT gene

✅ Answer: B — C228T and C250T mutations create new ETS/GABP binding sites, increasing TERT transcription and telomerase activity.

34. ALT (Alternative Lengthening of Telomeres) uses:

  • A) Telomerase
  • B) Homologous recombination
  • C) Translesion synthesis
  • D) Non-homologous end joining

✅ Answer: B — ALT uses homologous recombination between telomeric sequences to maintain telomeres without telomerase. It results in highly heterogeneous telomere lengths.

35. The ATR-CHK1 checkpoint is activated by:

  • A) Double-strand breaks
  • B) Excessive single-stranded DNA at stalled replication forks
  • C) Unattached kinetochores
  • D) Telomere shortening

✅ Answer: B — ATR is activated by RPA-coated ssDNA, which accumulates at stalled replication forks. ATRIP binds RPA-ssDNA and recruits ATR.

36. Translesion synthesis differs from template switching in that:

  • A) TLS is error-free; template switching is error-prone
  • B) TLS uses specialized polymerases; template switching uses the sister chromatid
  • C) TLS requires PCNA; template switching does not
  • D) TLS only occurs in bacteria

✅ Answer: B — Translesion synthesis uses low-fidelity specialized polymerases (Pol η, ι, κ) to bypass lesions (error-prone). Template switching uses the intact sister chromatid as template (error-free).

37. Common fragile sites in cancer are:

  • A) Regions where tumor suppressors are located
  • B) Large, gene-rich regions prone to breakage under replication stress
  • C) Regions where telomeres are located
  • D) Origins of replication

✅ Answer: B — Common fragile sites are large genomic regions (>1 Mb genes) that are difficult to replicate. They show gaps/breaks under replication stress and are frequent breakpoints in cancer.

38. Oncogene-induced replication stress is caused by:

  • A) Telomere shortening
  • B) Excessive origin firing, transcription-replication conflicts, and dNTP imbalance
  • C) Loss of p53
  • D) Telomerase activation

✅ Answer: B — Oncogenes (Ras, Myc, Cyclin E) cause aberrant origin firing, increase transcription, and create dNTP imbalances, all contributing to replication stress.

39. BRCA1/BRCA2 mutations increase cancer risk because they impair:

  • A) Mismatch repair
  • B) Base excision repair
  • C) Homologous recombination
  • D) Nucleotide excision repair

✅ Answer: C — BRCA1 and BRCA2 are essential for homologous recombination. Loss of HR → genome instability → cancer predisposition (breast, ovarian).

40. PARP inhibitors exploit synthetic lethality in:

  • A) All cancer cells
  • B) BRCA1/BRCA2-mutant cancer cells
  • C) Telomerase-positive cancer cells
  • D) MSI-High cancer cells

✅ Answer: B — PARP inhibitors prevent SSB repair → SSBs converted to DSBs during replication → in BRCA-deficient cells (no HR), DSBs cannot be repaired → cell death. Normal cells survive because HR is intact.
