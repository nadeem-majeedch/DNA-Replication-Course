## Case Study 1: The Young Cancer Patient

**Clinical Presentation:**
A 28-year-old woman presents with early-onset colorectal cancer. Her family history includes:
- Maternal aunt: ovarian cancer (age 35)
- Maternal grandmother: endometrial cancer (age 50)
- Brother: gastric cancer (age 30)

**Laboratory Findings:**
- Tumor biopsy: MSI-High
- MLH1 immunohistochemistry: negative in tumor
- MLH1 promoter methylation: negative
- Germline sequencing: MLH1 c.676C>T (p.Arg226*)

**Questions:**
1. What is the most likely diagnosis? Explain the molecular basis.
2. Why is MLH1 promoter methylation negative? What is the significance?
3. What is the recurrence risk for the patient's children?
4. What surveillance recommendations would you make for the patient's siblings?
5. What immunotherapy might be effective and why?

- Bone marrow failure (pancytopenia)

- Very short telomeres (<3 kb; normal: 8-12 kb)

**Genetic Testing:**

**Questions:**

## Case Study 3: The Replication Mutant

**Research Scenario:**
A researcher isolates an *E. coli* mutant with the following phenotype:
- Extremely high mutation rate (100-fold increase)
- Okazaki fragments are normal size (1-2 kb)
- Leading strand synthesis appears normal
- Lagging strand synthesis shows gaps between Okazaki fragments
- No growth on minimal media

**Questions:**
1. Which protein is likely mutated? Explain your reasoning.
2. Why are Okazaki fragments normal size but gaps persist?
3. Why is the mutation rate increased?
4. Design an experiment to confirm your hypothesis.
5. What would happen if you introduced a wild-type copy of this gene on a plasmid?

## Case Study 6: The Replication Fork Analysis

**Experimental Data:**
A researcher performs DNA fiber analysis on cells treated with Drug Y:

| Condition | IdU track (μm) | CldU track (μm) | Pattern |
|-----------|----------------|-----------------|---------|
| Control | 11.0 ± 1.5 | 11.5 ± 1.2 | Symmetric |
| Drug Y (low) | 10.8 ± 1.4 | 5.2 ± 1.8 | Asymmetric |
| Drug Y (high) | 10.5 ± 1.3 | 2.1 ± 0.9 | Asymmetric |
| Drug Y + Rad51 inhibitor | 10.2 ± 1.5 | 0.5 ± 0.3 | Collapsed |

**Questions:**
1. What does the asymmetric pattern tell you about Drug Y's mechanism?
2. Why does adding a Rad51 inhibitor cause fork collapse?
3. What is the relationship between fork reversal and fork collapse?
4. Design an experiment to determine the specific target of Drug Y.
5. How would you predict Drug Y would work in BRCA-deficient cells?

## Case Study 8: The Origin of Replication Problem

**Research Scenario:**
A researcher deletes a specific origin (Ori-1) from a yeast chromosome:
- Wild type: Origin fires in early S phase
- ΔOri-1: Region replicates in late S phase from adjacent origins
- No growth defect at normal temperature
- Growth defect at 37°C
- Increased chromosome breaks at the deleted origin region

**Questions:**
1. Why does deletion of a single origin not prevent replication of that region?
2. Why is there a growth defect at high temperature?
3. Why are chromosome breaks increased at the deletion region?
4. What does this tell you about the relationship between origin usage and genome stability?
5. How might this relate to common fragile sites in human cells?

## Case Study 9: The DNA Polymerase Switch

**Experimental Data:**
A researcher creates temperature-sensitive mutations in yeast Pol ε and Pol δ:

| Mutation | Leading Strand | Lagging Strand | Viability |
|----------|---------------|----------------|-----------|
| pol2-ts (Pol ε) | Defective | Normal | Lethal at 37°C |
| pol3-ts (Pol δ) | Normal | Defective | Lethal at 37°C |
| pol2-K620A (no Pol ε-Pol δ interaction) | Slow | Slow | Slow growth |

**Questions:**
1. Why is loss of Pol ε lethal while loss of Pol δ might not be (in some contexts)?
2. What does the double-slow phenotype of the interaction mutant tell you?
3. How do cells compensate when one polymerase is partially impaired?
4. What would you predict for a pol2-ts;pol3-ts double mutant?
5. How does this experiment support the leading/lagging strand polymerase assignment?

## Instructor Answer Key Summary

### Case 1
1. **Lynch syndrome** — germline MLH1 mutation (pathogenic frameshift)
2. **Methylation negative** = hereditary (not sporadic); sporadic cases show MLH1 promoter methylation
3. **50%** risk for children (autosomal dominant)
4. **Annual colonoscopy from age 20-25**; endometrial screening
5. **Anti-PD-1** (pembrolizumab) — MSI-H = high neoantigen load

### Case 2
1. **Dyskeratosis congenita** (TERT mutation)
2. **Gly761Glu** affects TERT catalytic activity → reduced telomere extension
3. **Bone marrow** has high cell turnover → most sensitive to telomere shortening
4. **BMT may help** but conditioning regimen must be reduced (treosulfan-based)
5. **Links to normal aging** — telomere shortening is part of aging process

### Case 3
1. **Ligase mutant** — gaps between Okazaki fragments (normal fragments but not joined)
2. **Okazaki fragments synthesized normally** but ligase cannot seal nicks
3. **Gaps in DNA** → chromosome breaks during next replication → genome instability
4. **Complementation test** — introduce wild-type ligase gene on plasmid
5. **Should rescue** if plasmid expresses functional ligase

### Case 4
1. **PARP inhibitor** — causes DSBs during replication
2. **BRCA1-mutant cells already have HR defect** → PARP inhibition adds less toxicity (but actually more complex — BRCA1-WT cells show more sensitivity because Drug X specifically targets fork restart pathway)
3. **Not as monotherapy** — resistance develops; use in combination
4. **Combine with platinum chemotherapy** or immune checkpoint inhibitor
5. **γH2AX foci, BRCA1/2 status** as biomarkers

### Case 5
1. **ALT** (Alternative Lengthening of Telomeres)
2. **APBs** (ALT-associated PML bodies), **C-circles**, heterogeneous telomere length
3. **Homologous recombination** between telomeric sequences (vs. reverse transcriptase)
4. **No** — telomerase inhibitors won't work on telomerase-negative cells
5. **Target recombination** (ATR inhibitors, PARP inhibitors)

### Case 6
1. **Fork slowing** — CldU tracks shortened → drug inhibits fork progression
2. **Rad51 needed for fork restart** → without it, stalled forks collapse
3. **Fork reversal is protective** → prevents collapse; loss of reversal = collapse
4. **Genetic screen** — identify genes that rescue Drug Y sensitivity
5. **More sensitive** — BRCA-deficient cells already have fork restart defects

### Case 7
1. **Patient 1**: FEN1 or RNase H (primer processing defect)
2. **Patient 2**: DNA Ligase I (ligation defect)
3. **Patient 3**: MSH2/MLH1 (mismatch repair defect)
4. **Patient 1**: Potential cancer predisposition (FEN1 mutations linked to cancer)
5. **Patient 2**: LIG1 syndrome-like phenotype
6. **Patient 3**: Lynch syndrome-like phenotype

### Case 8
1. **Adjacent origins compensate** — origins can fire from nearby regions
2. **Higher temperature = slower fork progression** → origin deletion becomes critical
3. **Longer distance between origins** → fork stalling at difficult-to-replicate regions
4. **Origin usage correlates with genome stability** — loss increases fragility
5. **Common fragile sites may correspond to regions with few origins**

### Case 9
1. **Pol ε essential for leading strand** — no compensation possible
2. **Physical interaction coordinates polymerase switching** at the fork
3. **Remaining polymerase can partially compensate** (with reduced efficiency)
4. **Likely lethal** — loss of both leading and lagging strand polymerases
5. **Confirms Pol ε = leading, Pol δ = lagging** assignments

### Case 10
1. **MLH1 loss** → defective MMR → cannot repair replication errors → SNV accumulation
2. **LOH (loss of heterozygosity)** — second allele deleted → complete MMR loss
3. **TERT promoter mutation** → telomerase reactivation → telomere maintenance
4. **Aneuploidy** → chromosome instability from defective checkpoints
5. **Immunotherapy** (anti-PD-1 for MSI-H) + **PARP inhibitor** if HR-deficient
